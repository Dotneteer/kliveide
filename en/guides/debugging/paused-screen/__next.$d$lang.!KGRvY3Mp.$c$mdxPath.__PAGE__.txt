# The Paused Screen

When the machine pauses in the middle of a frame, the emulator screen shows a picture that is half
this frame and half the last one. Klive offers two ways to read it: the **beam position** overlay
marks where the raster beam is and which part is old, and the **instant screen** redraws the whole
picture from what the screen memory holds now. Use them when you time mid-frame effects &mdash;
border stripes, Copper splits, a palette change per line, a sprite updated before the beam reaches
it &mdash; or when you want to see what your code has just drawn.

*Applies to: the ZX Spectrum models (48K, 128K, +2A/+3, +2E/+3E, Pentagon, Scorpion, Timex), the ZX Spectrum Next and the HT-1080Z. The Cambridge Z88 has an LCD and no beam, and the ZX80/ZX81 picture is generated by the CPU itself.*

## The beam position

While the machine is paused &mdash; at a breakpoint, after a step, or after **Pause** &mdash; the
emulator screen shows:

![The paused ZX Spectrum Next screen with the beam on paper row 94: this frame's border stripes and Copper split above the beam line, the previous frame hatched below it, and the hover readout of a pixel near the bottom](/images/working-with-ide/beam-position.png)

- a **dashed line across the beam's row** and a tick at the pixel it is drawing;
- a **pill** with the beam's position: `line 158 · paper row 94 · hc 381 · HC 72,429` &mdash; the
  raster line, the row of the paper (or *top border*, *bottom border*), the position within the line,
  and the frame tact;
- the part of the picture **past the beam, hatched** and labelled *previous frame*.

The overlay disappears when the machine runs again.

### Why part of the picture is hatched

The emulator draws the screen as the beam does, a little at a time while the CPU runs. When the
machine stops in the middle of a frame, the screen holds two frames at once: **this frame up to the
beam**, and **the previous frame after it** &mdash; including pixels your program has already changed
in memory, because the beam has not reached them yet.

Without a mark, that second part looks like a bug in your program. So the overlay hatches it: what is
under the hatch is real, but it is last frame's. In the picture above, the border stripes do not line
up across the beam line for exactly this reason.

Before it draws the overlay, Klive also brings the paused picture **up to the beam**: everything the
beam has drawn this frame is on the screen, even the part the emulator had not caught up with yet. This
is done on a copy of the picture and changes nothing in the machine &mdash; running on, the frame is
drawn exactly as it would have been without a stop.

<Callout type="info">
With the [instant screen](#the-instant-screen) on, the paused picture is the whole screen drawn from
memory as it is now, so there is no "previous frame" part: the overlay draws only the beam line, and
the pill says *instant screen*.
</Callout>

### When the beam is off the screen

The beam spends part of every line, and some lines of every frame, outside the visible picture. Then
the overlay marks the edge it left and the pill names where it is:

- `HBLANK` &mdash; horizontal blanking, between the end of one row and the start of the next. The
  beam line marks the row just finished, with a marker at the right edge.
- `VBLANK · 12 lines to the top border` &mdash; vertical blanking, between the bottom of one picture
  and the top of the next. A line along the top (or bottom) edge marks it.

### How far is the beam from a pixel?

Point at the paused screen, and the overlay tells you when the beam gets there:

```
(432, 267) · line 283 · hc 312 · HC 129,360 · in 56,931 HC
```

the pixel, its raster line and position, its frame tact, and how many tacts until the beam draws it
&mdash; or **drawn** if it already has this frame. That is how long your code has to change something
before the beam reaches it.

### Units

The ZX Spectrum models count in **T-states** of the 3.5 MHz CPU clock (`T 26,189`); the position
within the line is called `tact`. The ZX Spectrum Next counts in **7 MHz pixel-clock units**, the
hardware's `HC` (two per 3.5 MHz T-state), and its position within the line is `hc`. The Next's line
and frame lengths change with its display timing (48K, 128K, +3, Pentagon, 50 or 60 Hz); the overlay
always uses the timing of the frame in progress.

The HT-1080Z counts in T-states of its 1.77 MHz clock, and one T-state is one whole character, 6
dots wide: a line is 112 T-states, of which slots 2 to 65 draw the 64 characters. Its "paper" is the
64 x 16 character screen. [HT-1080Z: how the screen is drawn](/en/under-the-hood/emulator-internals/ht1080z-video/)
explains its timing.

The [ULA & I/O panel](/en/guides/debugging/machine-state-panels/#ula--io)'s `RAS` and `POS` show the same raster line and position.

### The Copper

On the ZX Spectrum Next, when a [Copper breakpoint](/en/guides/next/next-breakpoints/) stops the machine, the
Copper's hit gets a second marker, labelled `Copper hit $001`. The
machine stops at the end of the CPU instruction during which the Copper hit its breakpoint, so the
Copper's position and the CPU's are a few pixels apart; the two markers keep them apart.

### Turning it on and off

The overlay is on by default. Turn it off or on with **Show/Hide the beam position while paused**
on the emulator toolbar, with **Show the beam position while paused** on the **Emulator** page of
[Settings](/en/guides/customizing/settings/), or with the [`beam`](/en/reference/commands/#beam)
command:

```
beam          ; says whether it is shown
beam off
beam on
```

## The instant screen

The ZX Spectrum draws its picture while the CPU runs, following the beam, so a byte written to the
screen memory appears only when the beam next passes it. When the machine pauses, drawing pauses
too: what your code has just written may not be on the screen yet.

Turn on **Turn on/off instant screen** on the emulator toolbar (or **Show the instant screen** on
the **Emulator** page of Settings). While the machine is paused, the screen is then drawn in full
from the current screen memory, as if the beam had drawn a whole frame at this moment. Turn it off
to see the beam-accurate picture again. The setting has no effect while the machine runs.
