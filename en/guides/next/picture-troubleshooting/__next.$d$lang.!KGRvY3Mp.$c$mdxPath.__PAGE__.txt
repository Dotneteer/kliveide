# Why Does My Next Picture Look Wrong?

A wrong ZX Spectrum Next picture rarely says where it went wrong. The pixel you see is the winner of
four layers, a priority mode, a palette and maybe a Copper split, and the registers behind it cannot
be read back by your program. This page is a short route through Klive's tools: first find which
layer drew the pixel, then open the view that shows that layer's state.

*Applies to: ZX Spectrum Next.*

## Start with the layer

<Steps>

### Pause where the picture is wrong

Run the program to the frame that shows the problem and pause it. Every tool below shows the exact
state of a paused machine.

### Turn on the Layers strip

Turn on **Settings › Emulator › Show the Layers strip**, or right-click the emulator's status bar and
choose the strip. The strip lists the layers in their priority order, top first.

### Probe the pixel

Click the strip's crosshair button and point at the wrong pixel. The probe names the layer that drew
it and **the rule that made it win**: the layer order, the Layer 2 priority bit, stencil, a blend or
the fallback colour. Click the pixel to open the inspector of the layer that owns it.

### Hide and solo

Click a layer's chip to hide it, or **S** to show it alone over a checkerboard. If the problem goes
away when one layer is hidden, that layer is the one to look at.

</Steps>

[Video layers](/en/guides/next/layers/) describes the strip, the probe and the Layers document in full.

## What to look for, by symptom

| What you see | Look in | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| A layer is missing entirely | [Layers document](/en/guides/next/layers/) | The layer's card: switched off by the program, an empty clip window, every pixel transparent, or hidden in Klive (the on-screen pill says so). |
| Layers are stacked in the wrong order | [Layers strip](/en/guides/next/layers/) | The priority mode before the chips (`SLU`, `LSU`, ...) and NextReg `$15`; under a Copper split the probe shows the mode for that line. |
| A sprite is missing or in the wrong place | [Sprite Inspector](/en/guides/next/sprite-inspector/) | The **note** column: visible bit clear, anchor not visible, outside the clip window, pattern all transparent, a stale fifth attribute byte. The sprite-space map shows where each sprite really is. |
| A sprite has the wrong shape or colours | [Sprite Inspector](/en/guides/next/sprite-inspector/), Patterns view | The pattern number, the 4-bit/8-bit reading of the slot (a slot read both ways is almost always a bug), the palette offset. |
| Tiles are garbage, or the ULA screen is corrupted | [Tilemap Inspector](/en/guides/next/tilemap-inspector/) | The diagnostics: the map or the tile definitions overlapping the ULA screen or each other, a table wrapping in bank 7, attribute-less mode with a two-byte map, 512-tile mode taking attribute bit 0. |
| The Layer 2 picture tears, flickers or shows the old frame | [Layer 2 Inspector](/en/guides/next/layer2-inspector/) | The **Displayed**, **Shadow** and **Write window** sources: writes going to the displayed bank, `$12` and `$13` equal, a write window mapped to the wrong 16K. |
| Layer 2 shows nothing, or only part of it | [Layer 2 Inspector](/en/guides/next/layer2-inspector/) | Banks past 2 MB, an empty clip window, every visible pixel transparent, a scroll reading past the layer. |
| Colours are wrong on one layer | [Next Palettes](/en/guides/next/next-registers/#the-next-palettes-panel) | Which of the device's two palettes is live (NextReg `$43`, or `$6B` for the tilemap), and the colour at the index the pixel uses. |
| A colour you expected to be transparent is drawn | [Next Palettes](/en/guides/next/next-registers/#the-next-palettes-panel) and the probe | The global transparency colour (`$14`) against the pixel's actual colour, and the sprite and tilemap transparency indexes. |
| The picture changes part-way down the screen | [Copper view](/en/guides/next/copper/) | The Copper list's `MOVE`s and the raster ruler: which line each `WAIT` acts on, and `WAIT`s marked as never matching. |
| Something rewrote a register and you do not know what | [Next breakpoints](/en/guides/next/next-breakpoints/) | Set a NextReg write breakpoint on the register (`bp-set nr:$15`), or a sprite attribute breakpoint on the sprite. |

<Callout type="info">
Hiding a layer in Klive changes only what the emulator shows, never what the machine does. A
forgotten toggle can look like a bug in your program: while any layer is hidden or soloed, a pill on
the screen says so.
</Callout>

## When the registers are the question

Many picture bugs are one wrong register write. The
[Next Registers panel](/en/guides/next/next-registers/) shows every NextReg's current and last
written value, decoded bit by bit, and the Next Memory Mapping panel shows which banks are paged where,
which matters when your code writes Layer 2 or the tilemap through the address space.
